# Domain Broker Research

## Dedicated Domain Brokers

| Broker | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| **Sedo** | One of the largest domain marketplaces globally. Brokerage service negotiates on your behalf. Commission ~10-15%. |
| **MediaOptions** | Boutique broker, strong reputation for high-value domains. End-to-end negotiation and escrow. |
| **Grit Brokerage** | Well-regarded, known for personalized service. |
| **VPN.com (Michael Gargiulo)** | Specializes in premium domain acquisitions. |

## Big Registrar/Provider Brokerage Services

| Provider | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **GoDaddy Domain Broker Service** | Reaches out to owner anonymously. Starts ~$70 service fee + commission. Accessible but not the most aggressive negotiation. |
| **Afternic (GoDaddy-owned)** | Marketplace that facilitates transactions well. |
| **Dan.com (GoDaddy-owned)** | Good for domains already listed; offers installment payments. |
| **Squadhelp** | Managed acquisition service, generally well-reviewed. |

## Key Recommendations

1. **Stay anonymous** — Use a broker so the seller doesn't know who's buying; disclosed buyer identity inflates prices.
2. **Use escrow** — Always use a service like **Escrow.com** (industry standard) to protect both parties.
3. **Check if already listed** — Before hiring a broker, check Sedo, Afternic, and Dan.com to see if the owner has listed it for sale.
4. **Commission** — Typically 10-15% of sale price, sometimes with a minimum fee. Negotiate upfront.
5. **Casual vs. high-value** — For sub-$5K purchases, GoDaddy's broker service is a reasonable starting point. For higher-stakes acquisitions, a dedicated broker (Sedo, MediaOptions) is worth the investment.
